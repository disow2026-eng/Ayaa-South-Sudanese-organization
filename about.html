Roots in Acholiland

Born of Chiefs, Raised on the Land of Magwi

Simon Ottaviano was born into a position of immense responsibility. His father, Ottaviano Oyat, is the Paramount Chief of the Acholi people in South Sudan — a hereditary leader in Magwi County, Eastern Equatoria, the ancestral homeland of the Acholi. Acholiland spans the border between South Sudan and Uganda, and the Acholi people carry a rich tradition of governance, community, and resilience.

Growing up in Magwi, Simon learned what it meant to serve a community from his earliest years. The Paramount Chief's household was a gathering place for disputes, decisions, and the care of the people. This deep sense of duty would follow Simon across oceans and decades.

The Acholi of South Sudan speak the Acholi language (part of the Luo language family), and Simon grew up trilingual — fluent in Acholi, Arabic, and English — a linguistic bridge between his people, the broader region, and the wider world.

The Great Journey

Fleeing War, Arriving in America — 1994

In 1994, civil war consumed South Sudan. Like so many of his generation — the "Lost Boys and Girls of Sudan" — Simon was forced to leave everything he knew: his father's compound, his language, his land. He arrived in the United States as a young man with little more than determination and the values his father had instilled in him.

America was not easy. But Simon did what the Acholi do — he persevered. He enrolled, he worked, he studied, and he earned. He became a United States citizen and chose the highest form of service available to him.

"The most important thing my father taught me was that a chief does not serve himself. He serves the people. I carried that to America."

— Simon Ottaviano, Founder
Service and Achievement

Soldier. Scholar. Builder.

Simon Ottaviano served six years in the United States Army — a testament to his commitment to the country that gave him refuge. Military service taught him discipline, leadership, and the understanding that sacrifice for others is the highest calling.

After his service, Simon channeled his ambition into education. He earned his MBA from Sullivan University in 2008, one of Kentucky's leading business schools. Armed with his degree, he built a successful professional career — 12 years at Anthem Blue Cross and Blue Shield, where he developed deep expertise in healthcare administration and community health.

He later became a licensed REALTOR at Semonin REALTORS, one of Kentucky's most respected real estate firms — bringing his business acumen to help Louisville families find homes, even as he quietly planned to build something far more important back in Magwi.

Simon settled in Louisville, Kentucky — a city with one of America's most significant South Sudanese diaspora communities. He became a pillar of that community, a connector between the old world and the new, between those who came before and those who arrived after.

The Sacrifice

He Sold His House to Build a School

The story of Ayaa Senior Secondary School is not a story of donors and grant applications. It is a story of one man's extraordinary personal sacrifice for his people.

In 2022, Simon Ottaviano made a decision that few would be willing to make: he sold his home in Louisville and cashed out his 401(k) retirement savings — the sum total of decades of American work and sacrifice — to fund the construction of a secondary school in Magwi Center.

He named it Ayaa Senior Secondary School, after his mother.

"I had to do it," Simon has said. "The children of Magwi have no future without education. I had the means — maybe just barely — and I had to use it. My mother's name will be there for the children forever."

Named for His Mother — Ayaa

The school's name — "Ayaa" — is the name of Simon's mother. In Acholi tradition, honoring one's mother is among the highest expressions of love and respect. Every student who walks through those doors carries her name with them.

Partnerships

WaterStep: Bringing Safe Water to Magwi

Access to clean water is not just a health issue — it is an educational issue. For decades, the people of Magwi have drawn water from the River Ayii, unaware of the invisible danger it carried: bilharzia (schistosomiasis), a parasitic disease that causes chronic illness, stunts development in children, and quietly robs communities of their potential.

AYAA has partnered with WaterStep — a Louisville, Kentucky nonprofit that deploys water purification technology to communities in need worldwide — to bring a clean water system to Ayaa Senior Secondary School and the village of Magwi.

This partnership represents something beautiful: two Louisville organizations, one rooted in the American Midwest and one rooted in the Acholi homeland, working together to give the children of South Sudan something every child deserves — the ability to drink a glass of water without fear.

Learn About Our Programs
About Bilharzia (Schistosomiasis)

Bilharzia is a parasitic disease caused by contact with freshwater contaminated with certain snail species. Common in sub-Saharan Africa, it causes chronic illness, anemia, and long-term organ damage. Children are especially vulnerable. The River Ayii in Magwi was historically a source of infection for the community.

What Clean Water Changes

Safe water access reduces school absenteeism, improves child nutrition, frees women and girls from hours of water-fetching duty, and fundamentally shifts community health outcomes within a single generation.
